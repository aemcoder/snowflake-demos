heading

Web publishing changed under us.

intro

From assembling pages out of shared parts to generating unique pages that share the same rules.

card-1.label

The old deal

card-1.title

Reuse everything. Build nothing new.

card-1.body

Implementation partners shipped a library — Hero, Card Grid, Accordion, Tabs — and marketers composed pages from the shelf. Sites got fast by getting boring. It worked.

card-2.label

What broke it

card-2.title

The cost of uniqueness collapsed.

card-2.body

AI agents now generate bespoke, production-quality HTML — scroll-driven motion, cinematic transitions, three-week designs in an hour — from a prompt and a brand guide. And designers know how to drive them.

card-3.label

Now

card-3.title

The component library was a workaround.

card-3.body

Every page can be its own page again — provided the brand keeps the rules. The library's job moves up a level: from shape to guardrails.

heading

Apple-grade craft, on every page.

intro

The Apple iPad page. The Spotify campaign that moves like a film. The Nike launch that feels nothing like a template. Six-figure projects with months of lead time — that ceiling is gone.

card-1.label

Expressiveness at scale

card-1.title

Every campaign carries the craft.

card-1.body

Not just hero activations that survive the annual budget. Every market launch, every product moment, every micro-site — distinct, on-brand, deserving of attention.

card-2.label

No handover

card-2.title

The seam is gone.

card-2.body

Designers work on the live page. Marketing edits in-context. Translations run in parallel. SEO tunes the real thing. Nothing waits on anything.

card-3.label

Control moves up a level

card-3.title

Tokens, not templates.

card-3.body

Type, color, spacing, assets — enforced at the platform. AI review at publish: brand, a11y, perf, legal. Markets get latitude; the brand keeps the non-negotiables.

heading

The refresh is the pitch.

body

Brands tire of their visual identity every 12–24 months. The impulse is real — but expensive enough that most settle for staying static between major rebrands. Bundling the refresh with a platform migration changes the math: one project, two outcomes — a modern stack and a modern identity. Performance, SEO, and LLM visibility ride along by default.

heading

Two tools. No development in between.

card-1.label

Stardust

card-1.title

Generation, with the brand pinned.

card-1.body

Crawls the live site, diagnoses design pitfalls, proposes fresh directions, regenerates every page under a new design system. Palette, typography, voice — pinned, so identity survives.

card-2.label

Snowflake

card-2.title

Any AI page → editable EDS site.

card-2.body

Drop AI-generated prototypes into a known folder; out comes a publishable EDS site with the authoring stack already wired: in-page editing, scheduling, drafts, edge routing.

card-3.label

Together

card-3.title

Pixel-perfect, by construction.

card-3.body

Stardust outputs clean, semantic markup. Snowflake maps it onto Edge Delivery's blocks without a hand-off step. The handoff to EDS isn't a migration — it's the same artifact, reframed.

heading

Ship the demo before the deck.

step-1

Hand-pick three brands across distinct segments — CPG, mobility, hospitality.

step-2

Run Stardust to produce side-by-side before/after previews — without involving the customer.

step-3

Present to brand and marketing leadership. Capture exec response and intent to proceed.

step-4

Convert interested brands into a Snowflake-led migration to AEM Edge Delivery.
